# Video reference notes

These notes capture the useful direction from the three references the user supplied. The embedded X media was not directly playable in the browser session, so the notes rely on the visible post copy, captions, and surrounding context. We are using the production principles rather than copying any creator's footage.

## MystiqueMide — Holdfill demo

Source: https://x.com/MystiqueMide/status/2103147209239724042?s=20

The post praises a Claude-produced product demo. The relevant lesson for ScopePay is product-first presentation: a short, polished demonstration can make a real product feel finished when the product remains the hero. We keep the live ScopePay screen visible and reserve motion for emphasis.

## Teevincs — MetaWin AI short

Source: https://x.com/fwteevincs/status/2103188565827944499?s=20

This reference is an AI short attached to a giveaway story. Its useful lesson is the emotional hook and fast visual rhythm. ScopePay should borrow the clarity of a single opening promise, not the giveaway framing or invented cinematic world. Our opening line is: `Work should be settled by what was delivered, not by who has more leverage.`

## Kenn Ronin — one-shot motion graphics and sound design

Source: https://x.com/kenn_ronin/status/2103603356686275034?s=20

The post describes a 15-second motion-graphics showreel with sound design. We adapt that technique as small, meaningful beats: a rule draw, a proof-card settle, a short confirmation tone, and a clean transition into the explorer receipt. We do not turn the 90-second product demo into a generic showreel.

## Applied to ScopePay

- Real interface first; no fake dashboards or generic blockchain b-roll.
- One sentence of narration per visual beat.
- Motion explains state changes: funded, submitted, released, disputed, resolved.
- Sound design is subordinate to the voice and never masks a transaction label.
- The final frame carries the live URL and proof language so judges know where to verify the claim.
